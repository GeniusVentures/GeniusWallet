## View all must NOT be re-targeted to Transactions. Banxa orders are not transactions.

Jakub asked for `View all` to open the Transactions tab with `Purchased`
preselected. **Traced from the code first, as instructed - the destination is
empty, and it is empty by construction, not by accident.**

**Evidence:**

1. `Filters.purchase` (`transactions_slim_view.dart:56,97`) matches exactly
   `tx.type == TransactionType.purchase`.
2. `TransactionType.purchase` is assigned in **two places in the entire repo**,
   both fixtures: `lib/dev/dev_mock_transactions.dart:128` and `:197`.
3. `grep -rn "Transaction" lib/banxa` returns **two hits**, both
   `TransactionStatus` colour lookups inside drawer content
   (`buy_success_drawer_content.dart:31`, `buy_cancelled_drawer_content.dart:25`).
   **No file under `lib/banxa/` constructs a `Transaction` or calls
   `addTransaction`.** `banxa_order_cubit.dart` has zero references to either.
4. `TransactionsCubit` is fed by exactly two doors: `loadInitial(walletAddress)`
   reading `TransactionStorageService`, and `addTransaction`. Its four
   non-dev callers are `handle_dapp_requests.dart:198`, `swap_screen.dart:353`,
   `bridge_screen.dart:346` and `dev_overrides.dart:65`. None is Banxa.
5. `dev_mock_transactions.dart` is imported by one file, `dev_tools_bubble.dart`,
   which is gated behind `kShowDevTools = bool.fromEnvironment('GW_DEV_TOOLS')`
   (`dev_flags.dart:15`).

**Therefore:** in any build without `--dart-define=GW_DEV_TOOLS=true`,
`Filters.purchase` matches zero transactions, always. Shipping the re-target
sends the user to the filtered-empty state - "No purchased transactions. You
have N transactions, but none match this filter." - which is worse than today's
behaviour, exactly as the brief predicted.

**So this plan does not build it.** `View all` keeps its `/buy/orders`
destination. Two things follow, and both are wanted:

- `/buy/orders` keeps its only entry point, so **nothing is orphaned by this
  task**. The orphaning the brief asked me to report becomes a *consequence of
  the re-target*, deferred with it, and it is recorded in the todo in Task 4
  rather than being filed as if it had already happened.
- The preselection plumbing is not built either. Building an `initialFilter`
  parameter with no consumer is dead API. The exact shape it would take is
  written into the todo so it is a short job the day it unblocks, not a
  re-investigation.

### The mechanism, recorded so it does not need re-deriving

It is simpler than the brief feared and there is only one source of truth to
thread. `/transactions` is `const TransactionsScreen()` (`router.dart:225`) ->
`TransactionsStream(page: true)` (`transactions_screen.dart:119`) ->
`TransactionsSlimView(page: true)`. The filter lives in exactly one place:
`_TransactionsSlimViewState.selectedFilter` (`transactions_slim_view.dart:185`),
default `Filters.all`, shared by the panel bar (`:306-309`) and the page rail
(`:387-393`). Preselection is a nullable `initialFilter` threaded through those
three widgets and seated in `initState`. No second source of truth, no cubit.

### What would have to exist first, and why it is a product decision

An order would have to become a `Transaction`. `OrderStatus`
(`banxa_model.dart:111-135`) has `orderId`, `status`, `createdAt`,
`cryptoCurrency`, `cryptoAmount`, `walletAddress`, `fiatAmount` - and **no
on-chain transaction hash**. `Transaction`'s `hash` is its identity:
`TransactionsCubit` dedupes with a `Set<Transaction>` and
`TransactionStorageService.removeTransaction` keys on `txHash`. A mapper would
have to mint a synthetic hash such as `banxa:$orderId`, which then decides
dedupe-on-refetch behaviour and whatever the row does with an explorer link.

That is a design decision about whether a fiat purchase belongs in the wallet's
on-chain history at all, and Jakub has not made it. It is not a quick task.
**Recommendation: ask Jakub the product question before anyone writes the
mapper.**

## Scheme B's labelled track does not fit on the kicker row at every width

Jakub's amendment 3 asks for "Your orders" ... [track] [View all] on one line.
The brief asked me to measure it against the app's real minimum rather than ship
an overflow. **It does not fit at the narrow end, and the sketch's 758px frame is
the app's widest case, not a typical one.**

**Real card widths, from the code:**

- Page padding is `EdgeInsets.fromLTRB(12, space32, 12, space8)`
  (`banxa_buy_screen.dart:400-403`), capped at `GeniusBreakpoints.xxl` (1536).
- At `constraints.maxWidth >= GeniusBreakpoints.large` (1024) the layout is
  `Row(Expanded(formCard), SizedBox(space8 = 16), Expanded(ordersRail))`
  (`:455-468`). Below 1024 they stack and the rail takes the full content width.
- `GWCard` default padding is `EdgeInsets.all(space8)` = **16px**, not the
  sketch's 20 (`gw_card.dart:13`).

| Window | Rail card outer | Inner content |
|--------|-----------------|---------------|
| 1024 (two-column minimum) | (1000 - 16) / 2 = 492 | **460** |
| 1536+ (capped, two-column) | (1536 - 16) / 2 = 760 | **728** |
| 1023 (stacked) | 999 | 967 |
| 400 (phone, stacked) | 376 | **344** |

The sketch's 758px is therefore reached only at the xxl cap. **The two-column
minimum gives 460px of inner width, and the phone case gives 344px.**

**Budget for the inline row** (estimates - the executor must measure, see below):
`Your orders` kicker approx 92 + minimum gap 16 + track approx 332 (the sketch's
own measurement, `index.html:361`) + gap `space6` 12 + `VIEW ALL` link approx 84
= **approximately 536px required**.

So the inline row needs roughly 536px of inner width. It **overflows at 460**
(the two-column minimum, which is the exact width where the layout branches) and
**overflows badly at 344** (phone). It fits from about 1136px of window upward in
the two-column layout.

**The fallback, and it is not a scope reduction.** Below the threshold the track
drops to its own row directly beneath the kicker - which is precisely scheme B as
Jakub approved it in the sketch ("Track row 40px + 16px above + 16px below",
`index.html:357`). Jakub's amendment moves it up onto the kicker row; that
amendment is honoured wherever it fits and the approved sketch layout is what
appears where it does not. Every status stays visible and every count stays
readable at both widths. Nothing is hidden, deferred or stubbed.

**The threshold constant is not to be trusted from this plan.** Task 3 measures
the three intrinsic widths in a widget test and the executor tunes the constant
to the measured value. Flutter's test framework throws on overflow, so the
no-overflow test at 1024 and at 1536 *is* the measurement, not a proxy for it.

<the_extraction_decision>

## Promote the track CONTAINER, not `_TransactionFilterBar`

The brief asked me to choose between promoting `_TransactionFilterBar` into
`lib/components/` and building the Banxa track from shared primitives, and to
justify it. **Neither, exactly: promote the container both existing tracks
hand-roll, and build the Banxa chips on it.**

**Why not promote `_TransactionFilterBar`:**

1. It is typed to `Filters` **and** to `TransactionBadgeKind` - `_FilterChip`
   calls `badgeSpec(filter.badgeKind!, gw)` (`:828`). `OrderStatusTone` has no
   badge kind. Generalising means generic over the filter type *and* the glyph
   source.
2. Jakub picked **B**, and B's chip is `label + count` at intrinsic width.
   `_FilterChip`'s doc comment locks the opposite: "Icon-only in every state at
   every width ... never by revealing a label" (`:763-766`). Reusing it means
   breaking its locked invariant. The sketch's Rule-of-Three argument was written
   for scheme A, whose chip *is* `_FilterChip`; B is the one scheme where the
   chip differs.
3. Roughly half of `_TransactionFilterBar`'s body is the divider plus
   `PopupMenuButton` overflow (`:623-633`, `:648-689`). Scheme B has no overflow
   by design - all four statuses are visible.

**What is genuinely shared** is the five-value container recipe that
`CONVENTIONS.md:358` fixes and that both tracks copy by hand:
`surfaceSunken` fill, `borderSubtle` hairline, `radiusPill`, `EdgeInsets.all(3)`,
`SizedBox(width: 2)` gaps.

So: `GWControlTrack` in `lib/components/`, owning exactly those five values and
the 2px inter-child gap, with **three** consumers - `GWTimeframeSegment`,
`_TransactionFilterBar` and the new orders track.

**Migrating the two existing tracks is part of Task 1 and is not optional.** A
shared container with one consumer is a fourth hand-rolled copy with a nicer
name, which is the precise defect this task exists to remove; and converging all
three is what turns CONVENTIONS.md's "they CHANGE TOGETHER" pairing rule from a
comment into something the compiler enforces. It also answers Jakub's
"uzyj tych komponentow, jak najwiecej" literally.

**Revert boundary:** Task 1 is self-contained and touches two heavily tested
files. If it destabilises the 902-test baseline, revert Task 1 alone; Tasks 2-4
can then build the orders track on a local container and the extraction becomes
its own follow-up. Do not let a container refactor take the header work down
with it.

</the_extraction_decision>
