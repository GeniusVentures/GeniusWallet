The picker lists the same symbol on several chains — ETH on 1, 137 and 80001, USDT on 1 and 137 — and the shipped row (032-A1) shows logo, name, symbol and balance with nothing naming the chain. Three rows can read identically and mean three different assets. This sketch asks how the chain becomes visible without breaking the list-picker archetype every other drawer shares.

The honest constraint: assets/json/networks/networks.json knows 10 chains with real icons, but the catalogue also carries 42161 (Arbitrum), 250 (Fantom), 1284 (Moonbeam) and 80001 (Mumbai), which it does not — getNetworkById answers 'Unknown'. Every variant here has to say so out loud rather than render a hole.

What it is A 17px chain crest sits on the bottom-right of the 36px token logo, ringed 2px in the panel colour so it reads as attached rather than overlapping. Row anatomy, height and rhythm are untouched — this is 032-A1 with one addition. An unrecognised chain gets a ? crest on a sunken fill, never a blank.
Why it might win It is the pattern every multi-chain wallet already teaches (Rainbow, MetaMask, Uniswap), so it needs no explaining. It costs zero vertical space, which matters when the list is 30 rows. In Flutter it is a Stack with one Positioned — the path of least resistance.
Where it hurts 17px is small: at a glance you see that there is a chain, not which. Two similar circular marks (matic purple / eth grey) are hard to tell apart in peripheral vision, and the ? crest can read as a broken image rather than a deliberate state.

What it is The second line stops being just the symbol and becomes symbol · chain, with a 12px chain icon inline. The chain is named in words, so there is nothing to decode. An unrecognised chain reads Chain 42161 in the warning colour — the id is real information, and admitting we do not know its name is more honest than a question mark.
Why it might win It is the only variant where the chain is readable rather than recognisable, which is what you want when two rows differ ONLY by chain. It also survives a wallet with no icon for a chain, because the name carries it. Cheapest to build: one Row inside the existing subtitle slot.
Where it hurts The subtitle line gets busy, and long chain names collide with long token names in a 420px panel — the ellipsis will land on the chain, which is the part you added it for. It also spends the row's only free text line, so a future addition (price, fiat value) has nowhere left to go.

What it is Rows keep 032-A1 exactly; the chain becomes the structure. Sticky section headers carry the chain icon, name and a count. Unrecognised chains fall into a final Unrecognised chains group rather than being scattered through the list pretending to be fine.
Why it might win It answers a question the other two cannot: what do I hold, per chain? Duplicate symbols stop competing because they are never adjacent. The sticky header means the chain is on screen even when the row that named it has scrolled away — the only variant where that is true.
Where it hurts It spends the most vertical space, on a list that is already capped at 30 rows. Search fights grouping: filtering to two tokens can leave two headers and two rows, which reads as ceremony. And a wallet holding one token per chain becomes all headers, no list.
