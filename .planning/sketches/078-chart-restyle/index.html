Rollout - where B goes, and what it costs to put it there

B was chosen on 2026-07-29. The question left open was whether the surfaces have room for it. Checking that turned up something that changes the shape of the answer: the dashboard chart does not have a height. ChartDashboardView has three call sites under three different height regimes (dashboard_screen.dart:223, :273, :303), and only one of them is the 350 cap the earlier analysis used. So "does B fit the dashboard" has no single answer, and no per-surface setting can give it one. The floor is 220px of plot: B spends 22px on the time-label row, and under roughly 198px of remaining graph the ladder collapses to 3 labels across an 8%-padded window, at which point the axis is decoration rather than a reference frame. ★ Recommendation - ship the two free surfaces, make the rest automatic, and do not touch the dashboard's padding yet.

1. B on the coin page (wide and stacked) and the Markets hero now. All three are free, none is blocked, and they are where somebody actually goes to read a chart.
2. Make A-versus-B a runtime rule, not a per-surface setting - the chart reads the box it was actually handed and picks B above 220px of plot, A below. That is not extra work invented here; it is already B's own documented reduction. It makes the dashboard correct at every window size without anyone choosing a number, and it is the only form of the fix that survives a layout with no minimum height.
3. Leave the dashboard's 48px alone for now. Trimming padding on a card that is filing RenderFlex overflows at boot is polishing something that is broken underneath. Once that card gets the floor height both todos ask for, rule 2 gives it B for free - and the 32px stops being a decision and becomes at most a small improvement to the window size at which B kicks in (1060px tall → 996px).

Runner-up: a cut-down B - drop the time-label row, narrow the gutter to 46, floor ticks at 3 - so every surface keeps the frame. Rejected because it produces a second chart language that looks like B but cannot be read like B, and it would ship exactly where a misread costs most. Rejected: B on the coin page only, leaving the dashboard and hero on today's always-mint chart. That keeps three chart languages in one product and leaves the failing borderStrong touched-spot indicator (2.10:1 against a 3:1 gate) in place.

What exists, what gets adapted, what must be built

Provenance legend: Existing = shipped, reused as-is · Adapted = shipped component or library feature, configuration changed · New = must be written. Every line below was read out of the tree at 8ed02e78, not inherited from a planning doc.

ItemStatusNeeded byEvidence
fl_chart 1.2.0 Existing all Grid, right-hand axis widgets, full-height crosshair, suppressible tooltip, extraLinesData labels and cutOffY two-tone fill are all in the installed version. No new dependency for any scheme.
chartYBounds Existing all crypto_live_chart.dart:66, wired at :139, 5 tests in test/chart/chart_y_bounds_test.dart. The 070/071 fix (ruler from the VISIBLE slice) is intact - every scheme reuses it verbatim.
GWCard, GWKicker Existing all The card and its PRICE label are already base components. Untouched by every scheme.
Trend colour on the line Adapted A B C D Wiring, not building. trendColor is already computed at crypto_live_chart.dart:319 - and reaches only the % pill (:430, :440). The line, its gradient and its dot take a constant mintColor at :464, :470, :512. The value exists; it just never arrives.
Touched-spot indicator colour Adapted A B C D One token swap at crypto_live_chart.dart:504: borderStrong (white 24%, 2.10:1) fails the 3:1 gate for a meaningful graphical object. borderControl is already in the palette at 3.30:1.
Area fill fade Adapted A B D Today's gradient runs edge to edge at 30% alpha (:470). Every scheme ramps it to zero by 62% of plot height, because a full fill reads as sitting on zero and chartYBounds is deliberately non-zero. C is exempt - its fill is bounded by the open price, a real reference.
Timeframe segment New A B C D Replaces the zoom/pan row. Two forks exist with different label arrays (1H/1D/1W/1M/1Y vs 24H/7D/30D/1Y) and different tracks. This sketch settles it: the dashboard's array and its surfaceSunken track win. Closes the pending unify-timeframe-segment-component todo. Unification work, and it is real.
Y-axis tick ladder New B, D Nice-number rounding that keeps the tick count inside 2-8 at any range. This is where a naive implementation breaks - a 1-2-5-10 ladder collapsed 6 ticks to 2 in this sketch's own browser sweep, and a stablecoin printed $1.00 four times.
Pinned header readout New A B The value follows the crosshair into the card header instead of a floating bubble. Small, but it is a new widget - and the current floating tooltip has to be suppressed, which fl_chart supports by returning nulls.
Direct high/low labels New A Anchored to their own points. extraLinesData gives the mechanism; the collision handling at narrow widths does not exist and is the fiddly part.
Baseline band New C BarAreaData.cutOffY / applyCutOffY give the split for free, so the fill is cheap. The dashed open-price line with its label is the new part.
Last-price rail + chip New D 64px gutter, pinned chip, dashed extension. The most new code of any scheme, for the scheme recommended against.

Read the "Needed by" column before picking. A needs 2 new pieces, B needs 2, C needs 1, D needs 2 - but C's one piece is the cheapest of the lot and B's tick ladder is the one with a proven failure mode. Nothing here needs a new dependency, and nothing here touches chartYBounds, which is the only part of this chart that has ever been correct.

Contrast, measured, both themes

Computed with the WCAG 2.x relative-luminance formula, alpha values composited over the card they sit on (dark card #0C0E14, light card #FFFFFF). Text targets 4.5:1 (SC 1.4.3). A graphical object that carries meaning targets 3:1 (SC 1.4.11). Gridlines carry no information the axis labels do not already carry, so they are decoration and are not gated - they are listed so the number is on the record.
