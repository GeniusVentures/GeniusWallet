189 - Crypto News on a phone: what the feed is when the grid stops working

The News tab was designed as a desktop magazine (sketches 100-102) and shipped unchanged to the phone. Real content: the 30 items CoinTelegraph's RSS returned on 9 Aug 2026, real headlines, real deks, real photos, real timestamps. Real tokens, real Inter, a real 390 x 844 phone at 1:1. Every scheme reports its measured page height, so "shorter" is a number rather than a claim.

Today - as shipped

The control, not a candidate. This is crypto_news_screen.dart rendered at phone width: hero, "Next up", then the grid at maxCrossAxisExtent: 360, which resolves to two columns of 181px - not the one wide column the 360 suggests.

What the phone actually gets

Nothing on this screen is broken. It is a good desktop page being asked to be a phone page, and three things break in that translation. All three are measured below, not asserted.

Defect 1 - the headline does not fit its own tile

titleMd is 16px and the tile gives it 147px of line. Two lines at 16px hold roughly 30 characters at this width. The median CoinTelegraph headline in today's feed is 62 characters; the longest is 76. Turn RULER on and read the second column: nearly every tile ends in an ellipsis, and a truncated headline is the one thing a news card exists to deliver.

Defect 2 - one object, three card languages

An article is drawn as a hero (photo 16:9 + 20px headline + dek + age), as a numbered row (no photo, 16px headline), and as a tile (photo 168 + 16px headline + dek + age). Three components - _HeroCard, _NextUpRow, _NewsGridCard - for one model with five fields. On desktop the three sit side by side and read as an editorial hierarchy. On a phone they are stacked vertically, so the hierarchy reads as three different lists.

Defect 3 - the page is six screens long

The measured height is in the meter under the phone. The scroll viewport is 628px. Nothing is wrong with a long feed; what is wrong is that the cost per article is 164px (two per 328px grid row) for a headline the tile then truncates.

One more, smaller

The header carries a refresh icon (_UpdatedStamp, :248). Its own comment says it exists because "pull-to-refresh alone is unreachable with a mouse". On a phone there is no mouse and RefreshIndicator is mounted at :174, so the icon is a desktop affordance drawn on a touchscreen. The stamp beside it is not - it is the only thing telling you whether the feed is stale. Toggle MOBILE HEADER on any scheme to see the stamp keep its job and the icon leave.

The data ceiling, stated once so no scheme pretends otherwise. NewsArticle has exactly five fields: title, link, description (-> dek), pubDate, imageUrl. The RSS carries no category, no author, no source, no read state. Any scheme offering topics has to derive them locally - scheme D does, and pays for it in the open.
A - digest

No photos at all. One panel, 30 headline rows, the app's own row rhythm. This is what the News tab looks like if it is built like every other list in the app.

A - the feed as a list

One DashboardScrollContainer holding search, a kicker, and 30 rows separated by Divider(borderSubtle) - structurally identical to what /assets shipped last night and what /transactions already does. The headline gets the full 348px of card content, so it stops truncating: two lines at 15px hold ~72 characters against a 62-character median.

What it costs

The photos. All of them. imageUrl stops being read, CachedNetworkImage and _NewsPhoto leave the file, and the feed stops being something you browse and becomes something you scan. That is a real loss and it is the whole argument against A: a news product without pictures reads like a log. It is also the only scheme where NO PHOTOS changes nothing, because there was never anything to lose - and the live feed does ship articles with a null imageUrl.

Take A if News is a utility. It is the shortest page, the fewest components, and the one that makes the tab indistinguishable in language from Assets and Activity. Reject it if you want people to look at the tab rather than consult it.
B - thumb list

One card language, photos kept. Every article is the same row: a 72px thumbnail, two lines of headline, one line of dek, the age. No hero, no "Next up", no grid.

B - the photo survives at a sixth of the price

The grid spends 164px of page per article to show a 181 x 168 photo. B spends ~97px to show a 72 x 72 one and gives the headline 264px of line instead of 147. The photo stops being the card and becomes what it is in every news app on a phone: an identifier.

The honest weakness

Uniformity cuts both ways. Thirty identical rows have no first item - nothing on the screen says "start here", and an editorial feed usually wants to. That is exactly the gap scheme C fills, and the reason C exists as a separate scheme rather than as B with a bigger first row.

Where the 72px comes from

Not invented: it is kGWRowIcon 38 doubled less the 4pt grid's nearest step. Set RULER on and compare against a CoinCardRow - same 8px wall, same 12px separator rhythm, a taller leading slot. A 16:9 crop at 96 x 54 was tried and rejected: it breaks the row's vertical rhythm against every other list in the app, which all use a square leading slot.

B is the cheapest honest fix. One component replaces three, the headline stops truncating, the photos stay, and the page drops by more than half. It is also the least interesting page in the set.
C - lead + digest  ★ chosen 2026-08-09

One big photo, then B. The lead story keeps the full-bleed hero - the page still has a face - and everything behind it is B's single row language inside one panel.

C - the page keeps a front page ★ chosen

The hero is the one thing in today's design that genuinely works on a phone: 378px wide, a 213px photo, a 20px headline with room to breathe. It is the grid that fails, not the hero. C keeps exactly one of them and converts the other 29 articles into B's rows.

What "Next up" was for, and why it goes

On a wide window "Next up" is the right-hand 2/5 of the hero band - it fills a column that would otherwise be empty. On a phone _bandBreakpoint 760 puts it underneath the hero (:310-325), where it is a numbered list of three headlines sitting between two other lists. It has no job there. Its three articles simply become the first three rows of the panel, which is where a reader looking for "what is next" would look anyway.

Two decisions inside C, both toggleable
  • Hero inside or outside a panel. It is drawn outside, as its own GWCard - the same thing News already does. Boxing it inside a second panel would be a card inside a card.
  • Search placement. Inside the panel at full card width, exactly where /assets puts it (assets_screen.dart:417). It is below the hero, so the first screen is the story, not the field - which is the reverse of today, where a search field you rarely use sits above the fold every time.
Recommended. It is the only scheme that keeps a first impression and still fixes all three defects, it deletes two of the three card components instead of all three, and its first screen is a photo and a headline rather than a photo and a search box.
D - C + topics

C with a way in. A topic track above the list and a "new since you last looked" divider. The chips filter live - tap Bitcoin and watch both the list and the count move.

D - navigation, and what it costs to be honest about it

C makes the feed readable but leaves it a single undifferentiated stream. D adds the two things a reader actually asks a news tab: show me the thing I care about, and what is new since I last looked. Both are buildable. Neither is free, and the price is different for each.

Topics - derived, not delivered

CoinTelegraph's RSS has no <category>. The chips here are a local RegExp over title + dek, run once per fetch on 30 items. On today's live feed the classifier lands like this - the counts under the chips in the phone are these numbers, computed in the page, not typed in:

The cost, stated plainly. A keyword classifier is wrong sometimes. "Trump's media company to terminate Crypto.com deal" is Regulation to a human and matches nothing here. Two mitigations are in the sketch and both are load-bearing: an Other chip so no article can become unreachable, and the chips are additive filters over a list that is complete without them - All is the default and one tap away. What must NOT ship is a chip row that silently hides articles it misfiled.
"New since you last looked" - one timestamp

This one is cheap and exact. Store lastOpenedAt in the existing Hive box next to the news cache (coinTelegraphTimestampBox already holds a string), stamp it on dispose, and compare pubDate against it. No per-article read state, no new box, no sync. Articles above the divider are new; the dot is brandPrimary. It is honest because it says what it measures - published since you were last here - not unread, which we cannot know.

Where the track goes, and what it must not be

Above the panel, on its own row, horizontally scrollable, 36px chips - not the 44px touchChipSize the transaction filters use. Reason: those are a bounded set of five that must fit the width; this is an unbounded scrolling row where the height is the vertical cost paid on every screen. Compare against sketch 188's track with RULER on: same sunken fill, same hairline, same pill radius, one size down.

Take D only after C is agreed. It is C plus two features, so if C is wrong D is wrong twice. If C ships, the divider is worth doing on its own - it is a timestamp - and the topics deserve their own decision with a look at a week of real feed, not one day of it.
