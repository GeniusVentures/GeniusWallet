187 - the Assets page: breadcrumb, boxes, and what the page actually is

Three whole schemes for /assets, each a complete answer rather than a menu of fixes. They differ on one question: does this page use boxes. All three drop the ‹ HOME back link, and all three fix the page frame. Real tokens, real Inter, a real 390 x 844 phone at 1:1, live sorting and search, and the three data states that decide it - including every balance at zero, which is what Jakub's wallet actually looks like.

Three findings that decide the two questions
1. /assets is already a bottom-bar tab. nav_destinations.dart:143-146 - mobileDestinations is Home, Assets, Swap, Activity, News (S7, awaiting the on-device walk). The ‹ HOME link was written when the only way in was View all from the dashboard. A tab does not have a back. Tapping Home is 5px lower on the same screen.
2. Assets is the one page-header screen that does not use the shared frame. It hardcodes space32 on top and horizontal: 12 at the sides (assets_screen.dart:292, :351). Every sibling calls GeniusBreakpoints.pageTitleGap / pageGutter, which on a phone are 24 and 6 (breakpoints.dart:32-41). So on a phone the Assets title sits 40px lower and 6px further in than the Transactions title. That is a frame bug, not a taste question, and all three schemes below fix it.
3. WRONG, corrected 2026-08-08 while building sketch 188. This finding read: boxes on Home are a dashboard device, not a page device - no full page uses DashboardScrollContainer, not Transactions, not Markets, not News. transactions_slim_view.dart:483 is the proof that it is false. The narrow-page branch of _page() wraps the transaction list in DashboardScrollContainer by name, so /transactions on a phone is already boxed. Markets below kMarketsTableMinWidth draws a GWCard per coin (markets_cards.dart:57) and News a GWCard per article (crypto_news_screen.dart:452). Assets is the only unboxed list in the app. The grep behind the original claim searched the two files that DEFINE the panels and missed the one that consumes it from a page. The container is still GWDecorations.surface / radiusLg / borderSubtle / space3, and the box still costs 14px of row width - that part stands. What does not stand is the conclusion drawn from it, so read A's and C's verdicts below against sketch 188, not against this page as first written.

Every scheme carries the same three data states. ZERO BALANCES is the default on purpose: a design that only looks right when funded is a design that looks broken on the wallet in Jakub's hand.

Scheme A - flat tab

Nothing is boxed. Rows sit straight on surfaceBase, separated by the borderSubtle rule the code already draws. The frame is pageGutter 6 and pageTitleGap 24, so the title lands on the same X and Y as Transactions.

Why the total stays in the header

It costs zero vertical space, and it is the pairing Home already renders. Moving it to a hero block, as sketch 177's rejected variant E did, puts a second title-sized element between the title and the first row - on a 390pt phone that is the difference between four rows above the fold and three.

What A does not solve

With every balance at zero the page is a title, a $0.00, and eight identical $0.00 rows. A is honest about that rather than dressing it up. If that emptiness is the thing that bothers you, B is the answer, not a tweak to A.

Contrast

Nothing new is introduced. textPrimary 19.4:1 and textSecondary 6.01:1 on surfaceBase; the 24h chip is statusSuccess 10.39:1 / statusError 5.90:1 on a 15% wash of itself, exactly as coin_card_row.dart:113-140 paints it today.

Recommendation WITHDRAWN 2026-08-08. It rested on the claim that the sibling tabs are flat. They are not (see the corrected finding 3, and sketch 188). A is still the smallest change and still the only scheme adding no component, but the sentence it was recommended on - it puts Assets in the same visual family as the three tabs it sits beside - is exactly backwards: A is what makes Assets the exception. Keep A only if the decision is to unbox all four tabs.
Scheme B - value card, flat list

One box, and it earns its edge. The summary is a different kind of thing from the list, so the border marks a real boundary. Below it the rows stay flat, exactly as in A.

B - the box marks a change of kind

The card holds three facts a list cannot: total value at 32px, the 24h move, and where the money is. The allocation bar is computed from the same balance x price the total is - no new data, no new fetch.

What the bar buys on an empty wallet

Nothing, and it must not pretend otherwise. At total 0 every weight is 0/0, so the bar renders as an inert surfaceSunken track with no legend - flip to ZERO BALANCES and watch it. A bar that split $0.00 into eight equal slices would be a lie drawn in brand colours.

The real cost

A new component nobody else uses yet, and a colour-per-coin mapping the app does not have. The eight colours here are tokens (statusSuccess, brandPrimary, brandTertiary, statusWarning, brandPrimaryStrong, statusNeutral), but the assignment is positional, which means the same coin can change colour when the order changes. Fixing that properly means a symbol→colour table, which is a decision this sketch does not make.

Runner-up. It answers a question the page does not answer today - not “what do I hold” but “how am I spread”. Worth building the day the wallet has funds in it. On a wallet at zero it is a beautifully drawn empty track.
Scheme C - boxed panels, like Home

Two boxes, the Home language exactly. Turn RULER on: the purple outlines are the card edges, and the gap between the card edge and the phone edge is the width this scheme spends.

The semantic objection, and what is left of it

As first written this read: on Home the box says “this is the top 5, there is more behind View all”, and here there is no more, so the same shape carries the opposite meaning. That argument only ever worked if pages were flat. They are not - /transactions boxes its list on a phone (transactions_slim_view.dart:483), and the box there means nothing of the kind. So the box does not say “there is more”; in this app it says “this is a list”, and Assets is the one list not saying it.

What C still costs, and it is unchanged

14px of row width, on a column measured clipping. Transactions pays exactly this today. The cost is real and it is one the app has already accepted on the neighbouring tab.

No longer rejected. On the corrected facts C is the consistent choice, not the imported one. Compare it against sketch 188 scheme C, which is this same language on Transactions - the two are one decision.
D - C without the boxes

C's structure, A's flatness. The total leaves the header and becomes a band of its own - the dashboard's band, verbatim - and nothing is boxed. Two toggles: the number's size, and whether the band gets a kicker over it.

D - the part of C that was doing the work

Jakub, after walking C: can you do version C but without the boxes? The answer is a fourth scheme, not a variation, because C's total block and A's total are different objects: A's is a 20px right-aligned pair in GWPageHeader.trailing, C's is a left-aligned band under the title. D keeps C's block and drops the border around it.

What it borrows from the dashboard, verbatim

_AssetsTotalBand (coins_screen.dart:530), the band Jakub walked and approved on 2026-08-07 as sketch 178 scheme A: numericHeadline 24/32 at w700, the percent in labelMd 13/18 beside it, space4 horizontal inset, and CrossAxisAlignment.center - which is the fix for his own complaint that the percentage is not centred on the number. Measured residual after that fix: 0.13px. Percent only, no dollar figure, exactly as shipped.

The one real question D raises

On the dashboard the panel title is titleLg 18 and the total is 24, so the number wins its block. On a page the title is headlineLg 24 - the same size as the number. Hence the TOTAL toggle:

Both are shipped tokens - no new size is invented either way. 32 is not sketch 177's rejected hero total: that one was rejected for being a second title-sized element, and the whole point here is that 32 is not title-sized, it is bigger, so it leads instead of competing.

The kicker

Off is the recommendation. A page whose title is "Assets" and whose first number is a dollar total does not need a line saying TOTAL VALUE - the dashboard panel does not carry one either. Turn it on if the band feels unlabelled once the wallet has funds in it.

What D costs against A

About 44px of vertical before the first row at the 32 setting, in exchange for a total that is read rather than glanced. Nothing else: no new component, no width lost, no second frame. Against C it costs nothing and returns the 14px of row width.

Recommended over C, and over A. It is the only scheme that gives the total the treatment Jakub already approved on the dashboard, in the place C put it, without paying C's border. Set TOTAL 32, KICKER off and compare it against A and C at 1:1.
