1. Why the tags are cut - it is a layout defect, not evidence

transaction_displays.dart:366-405. The title line is a Row with two Flexible children: the token title and the action chip. Flutter's Row hands each flex child an equal share of the free space and does not give the leftover back when a loose child turns out to be narrower. So the chip is capped at half the line no matter how short its word is.

44.5px is about six characters. Of the eight action strings _actionFor can return, the browser fits exactly two: Sent and Minted. Received, Swapped, Purchased, Escrow locked, Escrow released and Processing job are all clipped. Jakub reported four; the real answer is six of the eight strings the app can produce, on six of the eleven rows on the phone. Open the "Today (broken)" tab and read the measured table - it is filled in from scrollWidth vs clientWidth at render time.

There is a second, quieter waste in the same Row: the amount column is also an Expanded with flex 1, so it takes 113px whether it needs it or not. A typical − 0.0021 ETH needs about 90. The middle column is paying for slack the amount column is not using.

2. Can the badge carry it alone? The vocabulary is nine, not three

transaction_badge.dart:9-19. Nine kinds, each an 18px circle with an 11px knocked-out glyph, painted at right:-2, bottom:-2 on a 40px coin.

Six distinct fills for nine kinds. #64748B carries sent, escrow and swap; #0AD89C carries received and purchase. Colour therefore cannot be the discriminator - the 11px glyph is the entire signal. An arrow and a clock are learnable. A pickaxe, a server rack and a padlock at 11px, meaning mint, job and escrow, are not conventions anybody arrives already knowing.
3. The finding that decides it: on 6 of 9 rows the badge does not show the type at all

In txRowContent the badge is chosen status first:

OrderRule
1if (status == pending) badge = pending
2else if (failed || cancelled) badge = failed
3else badge = _badgeForType(type, isSent)
A pending mint shows a clock. A failed swap shows a cross. On any row that is not the happy path the badge has already spent itself on the status, and the type is not drawn anywhere in the icon. Delete the word and those rows say nothing about what was attempted - and those are exactly the rows a user opens the page to find.

Two defects, one line of code apart
  • exists Title line: both children are Flexible. The chip is intrinsically ~50-100px wide and is handed 52.5.
  • exists Row: the amount is Expanded, flex 1, so it claims 113px for content that needs ~90. That 23px never reaches the words that need it.
The chip's own fill is the third quiet problem. surfaceMenu #171A21 on the narrow page's surfaceBase #0B0D12 canvas measures 1.13:1. It is a container you cannot see. Whatever we do with the word, that box is buying nothing.
A - the chip takes its intrinsic width
A - the pure layout fix

One change: the chip's Flexible becomes a plain child, the title's Flexible stays. The title absorbs every pixel of shrink; the chip always shows its whole word. Nothing is removed and no copy changes.

Honest, minimal, and correct as far as it goes. It is the change I would make today if the answer had to be one line. Every action word becomes readable, which is literally what Jakub asked for first.
But it only moves the truncation. 113px cannot hold a token symbol and a fourteen-character action word at 16px and 13px. A does not create width; it chooses a different victim, and it chooses the headline. The arithmetic says the title line is the wrong home for two strings, whichever one you protect.
B - no chip, the badge carries it
B - Jakub's proposal, built and then stress-tested

Delete the chip. The title line is the token alone, the subtitle is unchanged, the 11px badge is the only statement of what happened. It is the calmest of the five phones, and that is a real argument. Nothing is clipped, the rows breathe, the scan column down the left is clean.

The killer is not the exotic kinds, it is the status override. Every pending and every failed row shows the status badge, so its type is drawn nowhere. Under B a failed swap and a failed send are one red cross and one address. That is 2 of the 11 rows on this phone, and in a real wallet it is every row that matters.
The accessibility position, stated precisely

SC 1.4.1 Use of Color is not the operative criterion and it would be sloppy to claim it is: the badges differ by glyph, not by colour alone. What 1.4.1 does catch is the fill collision - six fills for nine kinds means colour is never sufficient here, so the 11px glyph is load-bearing and may never be dropped for a coloured dot.

SC 1.1.1 Non-text Content is the one that applies, and B already passes it: TransactionBadge wraps itself in Semantics(label: spec.label), so VoiceOver announces "Mint". So B is conformant and still wrong - the cost is comprehension for sighted users, which no success criterion measures.

If B ships anyway, the text equivalent has to live somewhere visible. The honest place is the subtitle - which is scheme C. The detail drawer does not count: a label you must tap a row to read is not a label on the row.
C - the action leads the subtitle ★
C - move the word to the line that already half-says it

The title line becomes the token alone, exactly as in B. The action moves down to the subtitle and leads it. The subtitle is then three pieces with three different layout rules:

PieceFlexExample

And where the subtitle already carried the action, the copy is deduplicated rather than doubled: mint stays Minted to wallet, escrow stays Locked in escrow, purchase stays Card purchase, and the job row becomes Processing job 0x44ab…10ff instead of saying "job" twice. On those rows the fixed / flexible boundary falls inside the phrase - the verb (Minted, Locked, Released) is the piece that never shrinks and the qualifier (to wallet, in escrow) is the piece that may. Visually it is still one sentence; the verb is at w500 so the eye finds it without a second colour.

Flip to LONG TOKEN NAMES. The token still ellipsises - it is on a 113px line - but no action word is ever clipped in any state, because it is the one piece with no flex. Compare the readout with A's.

It answers both of Jakub's questions with one move. The truncation stops because the word is no longer competing for a 44.5px box, and the "is it necessary" question is answered with "yes, but not there" - the word survives for mint, job and escrow, and it survives the pending and failed status override, which is where B breaks.
It also removes the invisible chip. No surfaceMenu box at 1.13:1 pretending to be a container, no second background on a row that already has enough surfaces. The action becomes what it always was in practice: the first words of the context line.
The honest cost, and it is bigger than it looks in the readout. C clips eight context strings where today clips three. Do not read that as "C is worse": today's three are clipped alongside six clipped action labels, and an action word losing eight characters is a row that no longer says what happened, while an address losing two is an abbreviation of an abbreviation. That is why the readout colours the two differently rather than adding them up. The full address is one tap away in the detail drawer with a copy button; the action word has no such fallback.
Tap ADDRESS 3+3. The mitigation, if the double ellipsis on 0x7a3f…9c2… bothers you: when the action leads the line, the address abbreviation gives back the characters the action just took. WalletUtils.getAddressForDisplay is the single place that decides it, so this is one constant, not a new rule per surface. Watch the amber count drop.
It also fixes a truncation nobody has reported yet. On the Today phone, look at the Pending row: Minted to wallet · Pending is about 172px on a 113px line, so the status is already being clipped off the end today - the app is losing the word "Pending" on the one row where it matters most. C's three-piece rule pins the status to the right edge and lets the qualifier give way instead, so it reads Minted … · Pending. Less pretty, strictly more true.
And one real regression: the title line is now a single 16px w600 token symbol with a lot of air beside it. The row reads slightly emptier at the top. If that bothers Jakub, the title line is where the fiat value could eventually go - not in this change.
D - the action is the headline
D - my own: invert it, because the amount already names the token

The premise nobody has questioned is that the token earns the headline and the action is the guest. But look right: content.amount is − 12.5 GNUS - every row already prints its symbol in the amount column, at 14px tabular, on every single row, with no branch. The token symbol on the title line is therefore the string that is duplicated, not the action.

So D makes the action the 16px w600 headline and folds the token into the subtitle: GNUS · 0x7a3f…9c21. A swap's two-token title moves down whole (ETH → USDC · 0.25 ETH).

Scan the title column down the phone. It now reads as a verb list - Sent, Received, Minted, Processing job - which is genuinely how you look for one transaction. Then scan A's or C's, which read as a token list.
It gives the action the widest text slot on the row - the whole 113px title line, at 16px w600 - and it is the only scheme where the action outranks everything else instead of surviving on leftovers. Check the readout: the only string that still touches its limit is the fourteen-character Processing job, and that one is a copy problem (Processing would do) rather than a layout one. It also makes the badge honestly redundant, which is the strongest version of Jakub's own instinct.
And the readout is brutal: 11 of 11 context strings clipped. Folding the token onto line two means that line now carries token + context + status in 113px, so it truncates on every single row. D does not remove the crowding, it relocates it - and it relocates it onto the line that was previously the only uncrowded thing on the row.
Rejected for two more reasons. First, it re-opens sketch 010-A ("the asset is the headline, the action a quiet chip beside it") which was decided and shipped - and a redesign that silently reverses a locked decision is exactly the failure mode the quick-task regressions came from. Second, it destroys the token scan column: on a transactions page the question "what happened to my GNUS" is asked far more than "show me every send", and D makes the first question require reading line two of every row.
Worth keeping on the shelf for a filtered view. When the list is already filtered to one token, the token headline is pure repetition and D is strictly better. Not the default.

Where I land
★ Ship C - the action leads the subtitle. It is the only scheme that answers both halves of the question. The truncation stops for good, because the action word is the one piece on its line with no flex and can therefore never be the thing that shrinks. And the word survives in the three places B loses it: the kinds with no learnable icon (mint, job, escrow), the escrowRelease type that has no badge of its own at all, and every pending or failed row where the status has already spent the badge. It costs nothing new structurally - TxRowContent already carries action, subtitleBase and status as three separate fields, which is precisely the three-piece subtitle C needs. And it deletes a container that measures 1.13:1 against its own canvas.

Runner-up: A. If the answer has to be one line of Dart this week, delete the chip's Flexible and stop. Every word becomes readable, which is the literal complaint. Take A over C if you want the fix in today's build and want to think about the anatomy separately - but know that A chooses which string gets destroyed rather than creating room, and the string it sacrifices is the token headline that 010-A deliberately put first.

Rejected: B. Not because the instinct is wrong - the instinct that the row is saying too much is right, and B's phone is the calmest of the five. It is rejected on arithmetic: nine kinds, six fills, an 11px glyph, no convention for a pickaxe or a server rack, no glyph at all for escrowRelease, and a status-first badge rule that hides the type on exactly the rows a user opens the page to investigate. D is rejected too, and more firmly: it would work, but it silently reverses sketch 010-A's locked token-first decision and it costs the token scan column.

The answer to Jakub, in two sentences
The tags are cut because both children of the title line are Flexible, so the tag is capped at half the line no matter how short the word is - it is a bug, not a sign the word is surplus. But the badge cannot replace it either: there are nine badge kinds, only six colours, and on any pending or failed row the badge shows the status and stops showing the type at all - so the word should move down one line, where the subtitle is already saying half of it anyway.
