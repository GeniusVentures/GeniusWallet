018 · Job request flow — file → cost → confirm → in-flight → result

Where it renders
"Drawer" is not a pop-up. ResponsiveDrawer.show() already exists (lib/components/bottom_drawer/responsive_drawer.dart) and picks its own rendering from the viewport at GeniusBreakpoints.medium:
· Desktop (≥ medium) → showDialog + Align.centerRight: a 420 px panel pinned to the right edge, full height, rounded on its left side, dark barrier over the dashboard. Not centred, not a modal box.
· Mobile (< medium) → showModalBottomSheet: slides up from the bottom, isScrollControlled, drag-to-dismiss, safe-area aware.
Both wrap the content in the same Scaffold with a title bar, a close ✕ and an optional footer — so one body of step content serves all three surfaces below.
This is the flow that exists — just never shown as one. Every step below maps to a real call in submit_job_cubit.dart. Today all of it is one flat Scaffold: an "Upload" action in the app bar, a row of numbers, a raw JSON dump, a "Purchase" button, and a toast containing a transaction hash. Nothing tells the user there are stages, that money moves in two separate transactions, or what happens after.

What each step calls

StepReal call
1 FileopenFilePicker() — FilePicker, JSON only, single file. Shows a blocking ModalBarrier + "Preparing AI job…" while it runs.
2 CostrequestGeniusSDKCost(jobJson) → job cost in GNUS.
getBrigeOutGasCost(...) → chain gas for the bridge-out. Two separate numbers the current screen shows side by side with no explanation of which is which.

4 In flightbridgeOut(...) → tx hash, then requestGeniusSDKProcess(jobJson). Two operations, one button. If the bridge succeeds and the process call fails, the tokens are already burned.

All eleven arrive as toasts today — transient, dismissible, with no way to get back to the message once it fades. Steps 2–4 in all three variants render errors inline instead, where the thing that failed actually is.

Recommendation

A · Drawer with vertical steps, and /submit_job kept as the same content in a full-screen host for narrow viewports and deep links. Rec

Why a drawer at all: ResponsiveDrawer is already in the codebase and already used exactly this way for Receive (coins_screen.dart:170) — it is a bottom sheet on mobile and a side panel on desktop with no extra work. Zero new routes, zero new navigation state, and the Compute panel stays visible behind the dim, so when the job starts the user watches the progress land in the place it will live from then on.

Why vertical over horizontal (B): the steps have wildly unequal weight — step 2 is a cost table plus an optional JSON preview, step 3 is one sentence. A horizontal stepper allocates them equal visual space and forces a full swap of the body on every transition. Vertical lets completed steps collapse to a one-line summary ("style-transfer.json · 4.0 GNUS") that stays on screen — which matters when step 3 asks you to confirm spending money you decided on in step 2.

Why not C: progressive disclosure on a full screen is the closest to what ships today, and it is fine — but it needs a route, it hides the dashboard, and on desktop it wastes an entire screen on a five-field form. Keep it as the fallback host, not the primary.

The one thing all three must fix: step 4 is two money operations, not one. Show them as two lines with independent ticks, so a failure between them is legible rather than mysterious.
