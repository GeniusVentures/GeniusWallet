What is wrong - from the code, not from an impression

Five problems. Every one can be pointed at in a file, so none of them is a matter of taste.

1. Eight items in the bottom nav, three labels truncated. _allDestinations in responsive_overlay.dart:38-75 holds one list for both desktop and mobile. 390 px ÷ 8 = 48.7 px per tile. "Dashboard", "Transactions" and "Feedback" do not fit and end in an ellipsis - you can see it on your phone. Material states 3-5 destinations for BottomNavigationBar. Apple's minimum touch target is 44 px - we fit, but with no headroom.
2. The active tab can lie. _currentIndex() (responsive_overlay.dart:82-92) matches location.startsWith(dest.path) and ends in a silent return 0. The /buy and /token-info routes sit inside the ShellRoute but are not among the destinations - so standing on the buy form or on a coin page highlights Dashboard. With no warning.
3. The header is a desktop instrument squeezed into a phone. MobileOverlay (:470-500) puts _buildActionRowWidgets() - the same control track as on desktop: network, SDK account, wallet, Connect - into AppBar.actions wrapped in a horizontal SingleChildScrollView. Four fields and three separators in ~190 px. The title "Genius Wallet" eats the rest and carries no information at all.
4. Zero balance painted like a disabled control. coin_card_row.dart:126,134 - under noBalance the values go to gw.textPrimary38. Computed on surfaceBase #0B0D12: 3.54:1, below AA 4.5:1. Your MANIFEST says it outright: "a wallet with no funds is not a broken wallet" - and this code does exactly the opposite.
5. The FAB sits on the content. global_swap_fab_host.dart:124-128 sets bottom: 80 + bottomInset and right: 20. On a phone it overlaps the last asset row. A separate known bug: those 80 px are meant to clear a bottom nav that desktop never mounts at all - there is a todo for it from 2026-07-27.

The hard constraint on every scheme

All 8 destinations must stay reachable - that is criterion 2 from Phase 4 and the condition in the todo 2026-07-18-mobile-nav-ia-curate-bottom-nav.md. No variant removes anything; they differ only in where the overflow from the bar lands.

A · Curated Five cheapest

The bar comes down to five items, the four that overflow go into a "More" sheet. Nothing disappears. Click "More" in the mockup - the sheet works.

What changes

  • Bottom nav 5: Dashboard · Transactions · Swap · Markets · More. 78 px per tile instead of 48.7 - full labels, no ellipsis. Adapted
  • More = GWBottomSheet with News, Web, Feedback, Settings and an "Accounts and network" entry. The component already exists in lib/components/overlays/gw_bottom_sheet.dart. Existing
  • The header carries the wallet, not the words "Genius Wallet": name, shortened address, network chip, bell. The control track moves into the sheet. New
  • FAB removed - Swap is an item on the bar, so the floating button duplicated the route and covered the list. It also closes the 80 px bug. Adapted
  • Balance at the top, Compute drops below Assets. Compute is a feature for some users; the balance concerns everyone.
  • A quick-action rail Send / Receive / Buy / Compute using .chip in the control-track recipe. Adapted
Why this is the recommendation. It delivers exactly what the 2026-07-18 todo describes ("curate to most-used, overflow the rest") without inventing a new navigation language. The change touches one chrome file (responsive_overlay.dart) plus a sheet made from an existing component. Desktop stays untouched - _DesktopTopBar still reads the full list.
The cost. "More" is a bag with no hierarchy - four things of completely different weight (News, Web, Feedback, Settings) land on one flat list. If Web turns out to be used often, we have hidden it one tap too far and the composition of the five will have to be revisited.

B · Center Action

The bar stops being purely a list of routes. The centre button opens an action sheet - Send, Receive, Swap, Buy - because that is what a wallet user reaches for most often. Click (+).

What changes

  • Bottom nav 4 + a dock: Home · Markets · (+) · Activity · More. New
  • (+) is a two-level sheet: the upper grid is actions, the lower one is the rest of the destinations. Adapted
  • Swap stops being a tab and becomes an action - which is what it actually is. The /swap route is unchanged.
  • Balance in a hero card on surfaceElevated with a sheen. Existing
  • Compute as a strip at the bottom, not a section at the top. Adapted
The advantage. It is the only variant that answers "what can I do here", not just "where can I go". Send and Receive have no permanent place in navigation today - they sit in drawers inside screens. The dock lifts them to a permanent level.
The risk, and it is real. The dock is New from scratch: a new widget, its own focus/hover states, its own accessibility handling. On top of that it breaks the CTA rule from the project memory - "max one filled gradient per surface" - because the dock is filled with a gradient and competes with every CTA on screen. We would have to decide deliberately that the bar is exempt from that rule.

C · Wallet Header

The same bar as in A, but the AppBar disappears entirely. Wallet identity becomes part of the scrolling content - as in Revolut or Rainbow. Scroll the mockup to see the header ride away.

What changes

  • No AppBar at all. MobileOverlay stops mounting an AppBar; the page builds its own header through GWPageHeader. Adapted
  • +56 px of content - the bar's height returns to the asset list. On 844 px that is a visible difference: one more row above the fold.
  • The control track shrinks to two fields: the network chip and a kebab menu. SDK account, wallet and Connect move into the menu. Adapted
  • Receive / Send as a CTA pair under the balance - filled and outline, per the CTA weight rule. Existing
  • Bottom nav identical to A. Adapted
The advantage. The most vertical space recovered and the most wallet-like character. A header carrying the address and the network is genuinely useful; the words "Genius Wallet" carried nothing.
The cost, and it is not small. Every screen in the ShellRoute assumes today that Scaffold hands it an AppBar. Removing it touches all the routes in the shell, not just home: /transactions, /markets, /news, /swap, /logs, /settings, /token-info, /buy. Each one needs its own header or it is left with no title and no way back. This is the widest change of the four.

D · Segmented Home

The sharpest split: the bar carries only actions (Home, Swap, Activity), and every content screen - Assets, Compute, Markets, News - becomes a segment inside Home. Click the chips Assets / Compute / Markets / News.

What changes

  • Bottom nav 4: Home · Swap · Activity · More. 97.5 px per tile - the most of any variant. Adapted
  • Markets and News stop being routes on the bar and become segments of Home. The /markets and /news routes stay - only the way in changes. New
  • Segment = GWTimeframeSegment in the control-track recipe, the component already exists. Adapted
  • Compute stops being a section on home and gets its own segment - today it takes the top of the screen from everyone, including people who never use it.
The advantage. The only variant that genuinely answers "what is there too much of here", not just "where do we hide it". It separates two things that share one bar today: places you go to and things you look at.
The risk. Segments on home are a new navigation axis alongside the bar - the user has two mechanisms instead of one and has to learn that Markets is "in Home" rather than at the bottom. Deep links to /markets must now be able to set the segment, otherwise arriving from a notification lands on Assets. The most router work of the four variants.

Recommendation

★ A · Curated Five - with one borrowing from C

We go in with A, but take from C the shrinking of the control track to a network chip + menu. A is the only variant that delivers the solution already written in the todo ("curate to most-used, overflow the rest") without inventing a new navigation language - which means it can be verified on the phone in one cycle, instead of designing a dock or rewiring the router.

The cost, honestly: "More" stays a flat bag with no hierarchy, and the composition of the five is a guess - we have no usage data. If Web or News turn out to be frequent, we will revisit the composition. That is a 20-minute correction, not a redesign.

Runner-up: C · Wallet Header. The best design - it recovers 56 px of vertical space and gives a header that actually says something. It loses out for now purely on scope: removing the AppBar touches eight routes in the shell at once, so it is its own phase, not a step taken alongside navigation.

Rejected: B · Center Action. The dock is attractive and it solves a real gap (Send/Receive have no permanent place), but it is a new widget from scratch that also breaks the "one filled gradient per surface" rule - it competes with every CTA on screen. Come back to it once navigation has settled.

Contrast - computed, not estimated

Everything computed by the WCAG 2.x method (relative luminance) on the real backgrounds surfaceBase #0B0D12 and surfaceElevated #0C0E14. Threshold for text: 4.5:1, for large text and control edges: 3:1.

One real violation, shared by every variant. textPrimary38 on a zero balance gives 3.54:1 - almost a whole step short of AA. Lifting it to textPrimary80 gives 12.4:1 and is a change of one word on two lines. All four schemes include it, because it is a bug fix, not a design decision.

What this sketch does NOT settle

  • The composition of the five. I picked Dashboard/Transactions/Swap/Markets on the basis of what looks like the core of a wallet - not on usage data. If you have it, it beats my guess.
  • Light mode. Per the "dark first" rule I only computed contrast for dark. Light needs its own pass.
  • The real look with data. The mockups show a wallet with a zero balance, because that is your state. With real amounts the right column has to be checked for wrapping.
  • Android. Brian runs it; gestures and bar height may need their own decision.
