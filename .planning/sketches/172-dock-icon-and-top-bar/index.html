A · SWAP

The dock is swap. The same icon as today's FAB, the same route, zero new concepts - the floating button simply moves into the bar instead of covering the list.

What the dock does

  • One tap → the /swap route. No sheet, no intermediary. Existing
  • The icon is exactly Icons.swap_vert_rounded from gw_swap_fab.dart:73 - the user already knows it from the floating button. Existing
  • The global FAB goes away, because it would duplicate the dock. That also closes the bottom: 80 bug from global_swap_fab_host.dart:124-128. Adapted
Why this is the recommendation. It is the only variant that introduces no new concept at all - it moves a thing that already exists and already has its icon, from a place where it covers the content to a place where it does not. The risk of misreading it is close to zero, because the icon does not change meaning.
The cost. The dock is the strongest point on the bar, and swap gets it indefinitely. If it turns out that people send more often than they swap, we have given the best spot to a second-order feature. Send and Receive still have no home in navigation.

B · MOVE

The dock is "move funds". It opens a sheet with Send, Receive, Buy and Swap. It is the only one that gives Send and Receive a permanent place in navigation - today they have none.

What the dock does

  • Opens a sheet with four actions: Send, Receive, Buy, Swap. Click the dock in the mockup. Adapted
  • Send and Receive get their first permanent place in navigation. Today they are buttons inside screens - wallet_information.dart:176-185, coins_screen.dart:179,345 - so from home you cannot reach them without opening a coin first. New
  • The icon: two horizontal arrows in opposite directions - "move", not "swap". New
The advantage. The most real value per tap. It is the only variant that closes a documented gap rather than merely relocating an existing button.
The risk. Two arrows in opposite directions are dangerously close to the swap icon - and swap sits inside that sheet. If a user reads the dock as "swap", the sheet will surprise them. That is exactly the test the "Side by side" tab performs.

C · SCAN

The dock opens the QR scanner. A wallet standard: address, WalletConnect, payment. mobile_scanner is already in pubspec.yaml, but unused.

What the dock does

  • Opens a full-screen QR scanner: recipient address, WalletConnect pairing, payment. New
  • mobile_scanner: ^5.2.3 is in pubspec.yaml:35, but grep finds not one use in lib/. The dependency is already paid for. New
  • The repo only has components/qr/crypto_address_qr.dart - displaying a code, not reading one. Existing
The advantage. The QR icon is unambiguous - nobody will confuse it with anything else, and it is the only variant whose meaning does not have to be guessed.
The risk, and it is serious. There is no scanner. It has to be built: camera permissions, denial states, content parsing, error handling. This is not an icon change, it is a new screen with hardware access. On top of that mobile_scanner is one of the two reasons the iOS simulator does not work in this project - leaning harder on that dependency deepens the problem rather than reducing it.

D · GNUS

The dock carries the brand mark and opens Compute. It is the one thing no other wallet has - the dock turns it into a permanent entry point.

What the dock does

  • Carries the GNUS brand mark and opens Compute. New
  • Compute moves off home and into the dock - today it takes the top of the screen from everyone, including people who never use it. Adapted
The advantage. Compute is the one thing no other wallet has. The dock makes it a permanent entry point and gives the top of the home screen back to the balance.
The risk. A logo in the dock does not say what will happen when it is pressed - and the dock has no label. It is the only variant where the icon is a brand rather than an action, so it is the only one that has to be learned. On top of that a brand mark in a filled gradient collides with the one-gradient-per-surface rule harder than an action does, because it reads like the app's "home" button.

Four docks side by side, at the same 1:1 scale. Look at whether the icon reads without a label - the dock has none.

Recommendation

★ A · Swap - but only if we do not want to touch Send/Receive in this round

A is the only variant that introduces no new concept: the same icon (Icons.swap_vert_rounded), the same route, the same semantic contract as today's FAB. We move the button from a place where it covers the asset list to a place where it does not. Zero risk of misreading it.

But to be honest about where A is weak: it gives the strongest point on the bar to a feature that is not the most frequent one. If you have any hunch that people send more often than they swap, B is the better investment - it closes a documented gap instead of relocating an existing button.

Runner-up: B · Move. The most real value: Send and Receive get their first permanent place in navigation. It loses to A purely on icon legibility - two "move" arrows are dangerously close to the "swap" arrows, and swap sits in the middle of that sheet.

Rejected: C · Scan and D · GNUS. C has the clearest icon of them all, but there simply is no scanner - it is a new screen with camera access, permissions and denial states, and mobile_scanner is one of the two reasons the iOS simulator does not work here. D is the only one that puts a brand instead of an action in the dock, so it is the only one that has to be learned - and the dock has no label to help.

The no-label legibility test

The dock is the only element on the bar with no caption. The "Side by side" tab shows four docks in one row - if you have to stop and think about what any of them does, that is the answer to the question. My prediction, to be checked against your eye: C legible immediately, A legible because it is familiar from the FAB, B confused with A, D illegible without learning it.

What this sketch does NOT settle

  • The composition of the four remaining items (Home · Markets · Activity · More) - I carried it over from 171-B unchanged. If 171 settles differently, this comes back.
  • The "one filled gradient per surface" rule - the dock breaks it in all four variants. That is a decision to take once, not per icon.
  • The dock's behaviour on other screens - whether it is constant across the whole shell, or only on Home.
