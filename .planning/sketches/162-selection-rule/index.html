What decides how "selected" looks

The walkthrough was right: the nav bar and the filter rail read fine with an underline, and the drawer reads better with a fill. That is not an inconsistency to iron out - it is a rule the app has already been following without writing it down. The container decides the mark. Three containers exist, so three marks exist, and each one is already documented or shipped.

Why three marks is the right number, not a compromise

The instinct behind "one component everywhere" is sound - it is what stops an app looking like four designers. But the three marks are not three styles for one job. They are three answers to three different questions the user is being asked.

  • Underline answers "which one am I looking at". It sits on the edge of the item because the item continues to exist either way - a tab is still a tab when it is not active.
  • Filled chip answers "which position is the switch in". The track is a physical metaphor; the fill is the thing that moved.
  • Tint + check answers "which one did I pick out of many". It marks the whole object, and it is the only one of the three that has to survive a list of thirty.

Each is already built. Nothing new gets designed and one thing gets deleted - the slippage presets stop being a fourth variant and become the third user of a documented recipe.
