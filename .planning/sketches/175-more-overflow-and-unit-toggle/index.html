Try it: tap the wallet chip in the header, hit Reset, then tap the dots and choose Accounts. Same room, two doors, and the second one costs an extra tap.
Three findings, all code-grounded
1. Three unlike kinds of thing in one flat list. Destinations, utilities and identity, undifferentiated. No rule can be stated about what lives behind the dots, so no memory of it can form.
2. Accounts is duplicated. The header chip already calls AccountDrawer.show(context); the sheet row calls the identical method.
3. Built from the wrong component. GWSelectRow is a selection control - its job elsewhere is "pick one of these, here is a checkmark". Using it for navigation borrows semantics that do not match.

So the brief is not "redraw the sheet". Three of the four schemes take things out of it; only B keeps all five rows behind the dots. That is the axis to judge them on.

Why it is good

It gives the fourth slot an identity, which "···" can never have. It is also what Rainbow, Trust and Coinbase all do, so the muscle memory already exists.

But it doubles down on the duplication rather than removing it, and spends a whole tab on a screen most people open once a month.

D - tap More, the rail expands (no modal)
Taps: 0
D - the overflow becomes a control track

No sheet, no scrim, no modal. Tapping More expands a horizontal pill rail directly above the bar. That rail is GWControlTrack - the same surfaceSunken well with a hairline and pill radius used by the timeframe segment, the transaction filter bar, the Buy GNUS orders track and the Compute unit toggle.

Try it: tap More, then judge the chips by eye. Five across 342px is ~66px each with a 9.5px label. Would you hit those reliably with a thumb?
The case for it

On the literal question you asked - "ideally it fits the other components" - D wins outright. It is not a component resembling the others; it is the same component, its fifth consumer. It is also the fastest: nothing to dismiss, everything under the thumb, nothing occluded.

Five chips at ~66px with a 9.5px label is under the comfortable tap target and under the app's smallest shipping type. Add a sixth item and it breaks.
And it is still the dumping ground - a faster, prettier one with a hard ceiling. It fixes the presentation complaint completely and the structural one not at all.

You were right, and here is the number

The selected chip's fill against its track measures 1.15:1. WCAG 1.4.11 requires 3:1 for a visual indicator of state. surfaceMenu #171A21 on surfaceSunken #06080C are two nearly identical blacks. A measurable failure, not a preference.

U2 is the instructive failure: surfaceElevated is darker than surfaceMenu, so "lifting" the chip made it less visible. The lift-chip hover works on the page canvas, not inside a sunken well.

Decided: U1 BUILT 2026-08-06
Jakub chose U1 after seeing the alternatives and the maths. The recommendation had been U3, which satisfies both the contrast bar and the CTA weight rule. U1 is brighter still and the trade was made knowingly.
What it reverses, on the record. compute_panel.dart's _UnitSegment carried an explicit note: "no gradient in any state ... the Compute panel already has one filled commitment CTA (New processing job), and a unit selector must not carry the same visual weight." That surface now has TWO filled brand gradients. The recorded partner move, if the doubled weight ever reads wrong, is to demote "New processing job" to an outline button rather than quietly reverting the chip. The doc comment now says all of this.
Where I land
Overflow: ★ C, runner-up A, rejected B, wildcard D
★ C - delete the overflow. The defect is not that the sheet is ugly, it is that "···" promises nothing while holding three unlike kinds of thing, one of which already has a home elsewhere. C is the only scheme where every surface's contents follow from its name, and it introduces the fewest new components - the criterion you set. The gear is a chip shape the app already draws, all four routes already exist, and the settings screen is the accounts drawer's own construction.

Runner-up: A. Take it if Web turns out to matter daily, or if you want the profile tab every other wallet has. Safer and more conventional; it just keeps the account duplication.

Rejected: B. Most pixels, least structure, and a tile grid that exists nowhere else.

Wildcard: D. Wins "fits our components" outright by literally being GWControlTrack, but five chips at ~66px with 9.5px labels sit under the app's own floor and hard-cap what the overflow can hold.

Unit toggle: DECIDED - U1, built

See the toggle tab. Old chip was 1.15:1 and failed WCAG 1.4.11; it is now the brand gradient at 10.80 / 7.84:1.

The one question I still need answered
How often does anyone actually open Web? Under C it goes from one tap to three. If it is a daily surface, C is wrong and A is right. Nothing else here hinges on a fact I do not have. Click through C's gear → Web and A's Wallet → Web and compare how they feel.
