<objective>
Extract the account drawer from a private `State` method into a public entry point, so a second
surface can open it. No new UI.

Purpose: the compute panel's *not linked* state tells the user their selected wallet is not the one
connected to the compute node, and offers a way to switch. The mechanism to switch exists in full and
works - it is simply private, and mounted only in the top-bar action row. Without a public entry the
panel's affordance has no destination.

Output: `AccountDrawer.show(context)` returning the selected wallet, with the selection's real side
effects owned by the entry rather than by the caller.
</objective>

<task type="auto">
  <name>Task 1: Move the drawer, its rows and its two confirm flows into a public AccountDrawer</name>
  <files>lib/account/account_drawer.dart</files>
  <action>
Create `lib/account/account_drawer.dart` with a class exposing a single static
`Future<Wallet?> show(BuildContext context)`.

Move into it, from `lib/account/account_dropdown_selector.dart`: the drawer call at `:168-213`, the
row builder `_buildDrawerRow` at `:225-360`, the avatar builder `_buildAvatar` at `:362-383`, and
both confirm flows - `_confirmRenameWallet` at `:59-100` and `_confirmDeleteWallet` at `:102-163`.

**Move the confirm flows rather than passing them in as callbacks.** They are reachable from each
row's overflow menu at `:341` and `:351`. Leaving them behind would mean the caller has to supply two
callbacks it has no business knowing about, or the rows lose their menu when the drawer is opened
from the compute panel - which would make the same drawer behave differently depending on who opened
it. That is the failure mode to avoid, and it is why this is a move and not a parameterisation.

Because those two flows call `setState` and check `mounted`, the drawer body needs to be a small
`StatefulWidget` inside the new file. Their `setState` calls only maintain a local mirror of the
selected wallet, which the new owner holds anyway.

**Three couplings to break, and only three.**

The selected-wallet field. `_buildDrawerRow` is called at `:187-191` with the selection test
`wallets[i].walletName == selectedWallet?.walletName`, reading the selector's own `State` field. Read
it instead from `WalletDetailsCubit.state.selectedWallet` - the cubit is already read at `:166`, and
it is the same value `selectWallet` writes at `wallet_details_cubit.dart:119-122`, so the highlight
cannot desync from the selection. Note the shipped comparison is on wallet name, not address; keep
whichever field it uses today rather than silently changing the identity test.

The `setState` and the caller callback at `:219-220`. These stay behind in
`AccountDropdownSelector` - they are genuinely widget-local.

The two real side effects at `:221-222` - `walletCubit.selectWallet(selected)` and the Hive write of
the selected address. **These move into `AccountDrawer.show`.** They are the meaning of selecting a
wallet, and every caller wants them. Leaving them at the call site would mean the compute panel had
to know about a Hive box, and forgetting either one would silently stop wallet selection persisting
across restarts. Note that no migration is involved: the same box name and the same key.

**Preserve the root-navigator captures verbatim.** Both confirm flows capture
`Navigator.of(context, rootNavigator: true)` into a local *before* the await - `:65` and `:108`, each
with a comment explaining why. `ResponsiveDrawer.show` pushes on the root navigator by default
(`responsive_drawer.dart:99`), so a pop resolved from the caller's context reaches the shell's nested
navigator and pops the route instead of the drawer. `swap_settings_drawer.dart:52-63` records what
that cost when it was missed. Move the captures with their comments; do not rewrite them.

**Preserve the body inset opt-out.** The call passes a zero body inset at `:170-172` with a comment
explaining that the list owns the scrolling viewport, which is the single sanctioned reason to opt
out of the shell's inset (`responsive_drawer.dart:24-28`). Keep it and keep the comment.

Return the selected wallet so callers that care - the dropdown selector - can act on it, and callers
that do not - the compute panel - can ignore it.

Replace the body of `_showAccountDrawer()` at `lib/account/account_dropdown_selector.dart:165-222`
with a call to the new entry followed by only the widget-local work: await the entry, return early if
the result is null or unchanged, then `setState` the local mirror and fire the caller callback. The
cubit selection and the Hive write are no longer this method's job - they happen inside the entry.

Delete the moved members from this file: the two confirm flows, the row builder and the avatar
builder. Confirm nothing else in the file referenced them before deleting.

Write `test/account/account_drawer_show_test.dart` in the existing widget-test style used elsewhere
under `test/account/`. It must prove three things, which are the three ways this extraction can go
wrong silently:

That opening the drawer and tapping a wallet row returns that wallet from `show`.

That the selection reached `WalletDetailsCubit` - assert on the cubit's state after the tap, not on
the return value. A version that returns the right wallet but forgets to select it would pass a
weaker test and ship a drawer that does nothing.

That the persisted selection was written. Drive Hive through whatever setup the existing tests under
`test/` already use for it; if none exists, assert on the box contents directly after the tap. This
is the assertion that catches the regression with the longest feedback loop - a missing write only
shows up on the next app launch.

Add one guard test that the drawer opened from a bare context - one that is not the dropdown
selector's - still renders its rows. That is the actual new capability, and it is what the compute
panel will rely on.

| Threat ID | Category | Component | Severity | Disposition | Mitigation Plan |
|-----------|----------|-----------|----------|-------------|-----------------|
| T-14-11 | Tampering | The wallet selection side effects | high | mitigate | The cubit selection and the persistence write move together into the entry, so no caller can perform one without the other. A test asserts both after a row tap. Splitting them across caller and callee is how a selection silently fails to persist. |
| T-14-12 | Denial of service | Root-navigator pop from the confirm flows | medium | mitigate | The navigator captures at `:65` and `:108` move verbatim.
